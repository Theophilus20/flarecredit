Documentation

Everything you need to understand, verify, and use FlareCredit private credit scoring for XRP holders, built on Flare.

Overview

FlareCredit answers a question DeFi has never been able to ask: can you prove you're a good borrower without showing anyone your finances?

Today, lending protocols know nothing about you, so they demand more collateral than the loan is worth commonly 150%. That's capital sitting idle purely because trust can't be established. The obvious fix, publishing your transaction history, is one most people and every institution will refuse.

FlareCredit takes a third path. Your XRPL payment history is independently verified by Flare's Data Connector, handed privately to a confidential enclave, and reduced to a single signed number between 0 and 1000. That number goes on-chain. Your address, your transactions, and even the link between your XRPL and Flare wallets do not.

In one line: prove you're creditworthy without doxxing your wallet.

Core concepts

TermWhat it means here
Credit scoreA number from 0 to 1000 derived from your verified XRPL history, valid for 30 days, signed by the scoring enclave.
Identity bindingCryptographic proof that a Flare address and an XRPL address belong to the same person stored on-chain only as a hash.
AttestationAn FDC-issued Merkle proof that a specific XRPL transaction genuinely occurred.
Enclave (TEE)A Trusted Execution Environment where your data is processed in isolation. Even the operator can't read it.
Code hashA fingerprint of the exact enclave build. The registry accepts scores only from whitelisted hashes.
Collateral ratioHow much you must lock up to borrow. 150% by default; 120% at a score of 700 or above.

Getting started

You'll need two wallets and a few minutes. Everything runs on testnet, so nothing costs real money.

Prerequisites

  • MetaMask with the Coston2 network (chain ID 114) the app adds it automatically on connect.
  • C2FLR test tokens from the Coston2 faucet, for gas and collateral.
  • GemWallet, an XRPL browser wallet, funded on XRPL testnet.

The flow at a glance

  1. Connect MetaMask and link your XRPL identity (one signature, one transaction).
  2. Attest one or more XRPL payments you sent.
  3. Compute your score inside the enclave and register it on-chain.
  4. Deposit collateral and borrow FXRP at your score's collateral ratio.

Linking your identity

Before your history can count, you must prove the XRPL address is yours. This happens in three steps on the Identity page.

First, connect MetaMask this is the Flare address your score will be attached to. Second, click Sign with GemWallet. The backend issues a one-time challenge containing a random nonce, the chain ID, and the registry contract address; GemWallet signs it. The backend verifies both that the signature is valid and that the signing public key actually derives the XRPL address being claimed.

Third, click Link on Coston2. This stores keccak(flareAddress, xrplAddress, nonce) in the IdentityLinkRegistry contract. That hash is meaningless to observers only someone who already knows all three inputs can verify it, and the enclave is the only party that receives them.

Why a new nonce each time? Fresh nonces make each signature single-use, preventing replay. Re-linking without re-signing reuses your existing binding; re-signing mints a new one. Both are safe.

Unlinking and rebinding

You can unlink at any time from the Identity page, which calls unlink() on-chain. To bind a different XRPL address, switch wallets in GemWallet, re-sign, then link again.

Attesting history

An attestation is independent proof that an XRPL transaction happened. FlareCredit uses the FDC Payment attestation type.

On the Attestations page, paste the hash of an XRPL payment sent from your linked address. Behind the scenes: the backend asks an FDC verifier to encode the request, submits it to FdcHub with the required fee, and waits for the voting round to finalise. Around 100 independent data providers each check the transaction against their own XRPL nodes and commit a Merkle root of all confirmed attestations on-chain.

Once the round closes, the Merkle proof is fetched from the Data Availability layer and held in your browser. Expect this to take roughly two to three minutes.

Important: the payment must have been sent from the XRPL address you linked. Proofs for anyone else's transactions are rejected by the enclave and contribute nothing.

Getting your score

Click Compute private score and your collected proofs, plus the binding preimage, are sent to the scoring enclave. Inside the TEE, the enclave verifies each Merkle proof against the on-chain root, recomputes your binding hash and compares it to what the registry stores, applies the scoring model, and signs the result.

The signed envelope contains your address, the score, an expiry 30 days out, and the enclave's code hash. It's submitted to CreditRegistry, which recovers the signature and accepts it only if the signer matches the key whitelisted for that exact code hash.

Scores can be recomputed whenever you attest new history, or once the current score expires. Recomputing with an unchanged set of proofs is blocked it would only produce the same number.

Borrowing FXRP

The lending pool is collateralized: you lock C2FLR and borrow FXRP against it. Your score determines how much collateral is required.

borrowing power = (collateral value in USD ÷ collateral ratio) ÷ XRP price

With a score below 700 the ratio is 150%, so $150 of collateral supports $100 of debt. At 700 or above the ratio drops to 120% the same collateral now supports roughly 25% more borrowing. All values are priced live through FTSOv2 feeds.

Repaying builds your repayment record. Positions that fall below the required ratio can be liquidated, with a 5% bonus paid to the liquidator.

Architecture

FlareCredit has four moving parts, each mapped to a Flare protocol.

1 · Identity layer

A domain-bound challenge signed by the XRPL wallet, verified off-chain, committed on-chain as a hash in IdentityLinkRegistry.

2 · Attestation layer (FDC)

Payment attestations requested through FdcHub, with Merkle proofs retrieved from the DA layer and routed to the enclave rather than to a public verifier contract.

3 · Scoring layer (FCC)

A Go extension running in GCP Confidential Space. It performs proof verification, binding verification, and scoring, then signs keccak(subject, score, expiry, codeHash) with its attested key.

4 · Consumption layer (FTSO + FAssets)

FxrpLendingPool reads the score from CreditRegistry to set collateral ratios and prices everything through FTSOv2, lending FXRP brought over by FAssets.

Scoring model

Version 1 is deliberately simple and fully explainable you can always see exactly why your score is what it is, factor by factor, in the app.

FactorMaxHow it's earned
Base400Granted once a valid enclave-signed score exists.
Transaction history20040 points per unique attested payment.
Volume20035 points per 25 XRP moved, capped at 100 XRP per counterparty.
Wallet age100Granted when the oldest attested transaction is more than 30 days old.
Repayment record100Clean repayment history in the FlareCredit lending pool.

The maximum is 1000, and 700 is the threshold that unlocks the reduced collateral ratio. Every score carries a 30-day validity window, after which the pool falls back to the default ratio until you recompute.

Privacy model

Privacy in FlareCredit is structural, not a promise. Here is precisely what each party can see.

PartyCan seeCannot see
Public chainBinding hashes, scores, Merkle roots, pool positionsYour XRPL address, your transactions, the wallet mapping
BackendAddresses and proofs in transitNothing it could forge it holds no TEE key
EnclaveEverything needed to score (but its exact code is publicly verifiable)
Lending poolYour score and its validityAny detail of the history behind it

The binding preimage your XRPL address and nonce is stored only in your browser and transmitted only to the enclave.

Security & anti-gaming

A credit score that can be farmed is worthless to a lender, so the enclave enforces strict rules on what counts.

  • Ownership a proof counts only if the payment was sent from your bound XRPL address. Borrowing a whale's transaction hash scores nothing.
  • Merkle verification every proof is checked against the on-chain FDC root before scoring.
  • Deduplication the same transaction counts once, regardless of how many times it's submitted.
  • No self-payments transfers where sender and receiver match are discarded.
  • Per-counterparty volume cap ping-ponging funds between two wallets saturates a single cap instead of inflating volume.
  • Wallet-age gate a freshly created wallet cannot instantly earn age points.
  • Attested signatures CreditRegistry accepts scores only from the key whitelisted for a specific enclave code hash; tampered signatures revert.
  • Domain-bound challenges identity signatures commit to the chain ID and registry address, so they cannot be replayed elsewhere.

Known limitation: because bindings are hashed, one XRPL history could back more than one Flare address. Mitigations sealed enclave state or a uniqueness registry are a privacy trade-off currently under evaluation.

FAQ

Does anyone see my XRPL address?

Only the scoring enclave, which processes it in isolation. Nothing on-chain reveals it, and no observer can derive it from the stored hash.

Why do I still need to deposit collateral?

FlareCredit is a step toward undercollateralized lending, not a leap. A score reduces how much collateral you need from 150% to 120% rather than removing it. Fully unsecured lending would require off-chain recourse that no anonymous protocol can enforce today.

Why did my score go down?

Scores expire after 30 days. If proofs were rejected a foreign sender, a duplicate, or a failed payment the app lists the reason next to your result.

Can I use a different XRPL wallet later?

Yes. Unlink, switch wallets in GemWallet, re-sign, and link again.

Is this on mainnet?

Not yet. FlareCredit currently runs on Coston2 testnet. Mainnet requires the production Confidential Space deployment and an audit.
